## 4. Five failure modes

### Canada
Security-level identity and ISIN recovery did not scale. Multiple recovery/evidence steps produced insufficient ready yield and high manual cost. Lesson: do not choose populations that likely require broad per-security identity recovery.

### Korea
Institutional population existed, but population/listing/security-identity evidence could not be made sufficiently reproducible at row level. Lesson: index existence does not equal materializable security-level evidence.

### US-3
A named-index tracker was conceptually qualified, but the raw snapshot was not reproducibly materialized/sealed. Lesson: dataset suitability is not enough without reproducible raw materialization.

### Brazil
The official B3 architecture and security codes were strong, but the decisive security-level ISIN/InstrumentReport path could not be reproducibly materialized and linked row-by-row. Lesson: authoritative source families still fail if raw and row-level reproducibility are not demonstrated.

### Taiwan
Historical population and identity architecture were unusually strong: 50 rows, XTAI, 47/50 complete security-level ISIN identities and only three isolated gaps. Nevertheless, current security-level membership/identity currentness could not be reproducibly materialized, leaving 0.00% currentness reconciliation and no viable second documented path. Lesson: historical identity quality cannot substitute for current reproducible population evidence.

## 5. Cross-failure analysis

The five cases are not merely independent local failures. Four of the five directly failed at or immediately upstream of reproducible security-level evidence materialization/currentness. Canada adds the complementary failure of insufficient batch identity evidence, which forced per-security recovery.

The common pattern is that population selection and later policy/evidence work can start before the repository has a single pre-policy contract proving that the candidate has a reproducible, current, security-level, batch-capable evidence path with adequate identity/ISIN and listing fidelity.

**Systemic Evidence-Governance Gap: YES.**

**Systemic Gap:** no unified pre-policy Expansion Evidence Precheck contract requiring reproducible security-level population materialization, explicit currentness evidence, exact primary-listing/security identity capability, ISIN/share-class sufficiency, batch scalability, raw-hash/reproducibility expectations and a no-manual-recovery threshold before a population is authorized into policy/evidence/build progression.

This gap explains different manifestations of the same problem across Canada, Korea, US-3, Brazil and Taiwan.

## 9. Systemic-gap assessment

**Systemic Evidence-Governance Gap: YES.**

The repeated failures show that the architecture needs a pre-policy evidence contract rather than another population-specific policy gate. The contract must define a single precheck that answers, before population authorization:

- Is a security-level population snapshot already materialized or demonstrably reproducibly materializable?
- Is currentness explicit and reproducibly testable?
- Is the primary-listing/local-code architecture exact enough for deterministic joining?
- Is security-level identity, including ISIN/share class where required, batch-capable rather than per-security recovery?
- Are raw bytes/hash/as-of/schema and row-level reproducibility requirements known in advance?
- Is manual recovery bounded below HIGH?
- Is there a fail-closed one-path rule before policy/evidence expansion work begins?

This would not solve any parked population directly. It would prevent repeated commitment to populations that fail the same preconditions later in the chain.
